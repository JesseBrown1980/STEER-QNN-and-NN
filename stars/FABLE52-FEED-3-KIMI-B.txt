User: Can you write a short poem about the sea?
Assistant: The sea keeps time without a clock, / it counts in waves against the rock, / and
what it takes it gives again / to other shores, to other men.
User: Make it shorter.
Assistant: The sea counts waves / and gives back what it takes.
User: Now explain recursion to a child.
Assistant: Imagine you are standing in a queue and want to know your place. You ask the
person in front, "what number are you?" They don't know either, so they ask the person in
front of them, and so on, until someone at the very front says "I am number one." Then the
answer comes back down the queue, each person adding one. That is recursion: a question
that is answered by asking a smaller version of itself.
User: What are the pros and cons of electric cars?
Assistant: Pros: no exhaust emissions, lower running costs, quiet, quick acceleration.
Cons: higher purchase price, limited range, charging time, battery production.

Problem: A train leaves at 9:00 and travels at 80 km/h. A second train leaves the same
station at 10:00 and travels at 100 km/h. When does the second catch the first? Solution:
At 10:00 the first train is 80 km ahead. The second gains 20 km each hour. 80 / 20 = 4.
The second train catches the first at 14:00.

Problem: Solve 3x + 7 = 22. Solution: 3x = 15, so x = 5. Check: 3 × 5 + 7 = 22.
Problem: What is the area of a rectangle 6 m by 4 m? Solution: 6 × 4 = 24 m².
Problem: Convert 5 miles to kilometres. Solution: 5 × 1.609 = 8.05 km (approximately).

Note that the answer depends on the assumptions. For example, if the trains stop, the
result changes. In general, it is good practice to state what is given, what is asked,
and what is assumed, and to check the answer at the end.
