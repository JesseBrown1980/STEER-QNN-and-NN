There is a kind of tiredness that sleep does not help, and a kind of rest that is not
sleep. My grandfather knew the second kind. He would sit on the step in the evening with
his hands on his knees and look at nothing for an hour, and get up as fresh as morning.
"What do you think about?" I asked him once. "I don't," he said. "That's the trick of it."

The meeting began at nine and by eleven nothing had been decided. The chairman wanted a
vote; the treasurer wanted the figures; the secretary wanted to go home. In the end they
agreed to meet again on Thursday, which is what they had agreed the week before.

La nuit tombait quand nous sommes arrivés au village. Il n'y avait personne dans la rue.
Une seule fenêtre était éclairée, celle du boulanger, qui travaillait déjà pour le
lendemain. Nous avons frappé à la porte. « Entrez, a-t-il dit, il fait froid dehors. »

A noite caía quando chegámos à aldeia. Não havia ninguém na rua. Só uma janela estava
iluminada, a do padeiro, que já trabalhava para o dia seguinte. Batemos à porta. «Entrem,
disse ele, está frio lá fora.» O pão ainda estava quente e ele não quis dinheiro.

Caía la noche cuando llegamos al pueblo. No había nadie en la calle. Solo una ventana
estaba iluminada, la del panadero, que ya trabajaba para el día siguiente. Llamamos a la
puerta. «Pasen, dijo, hace frío fuera.»

Es wurde Nacht, als wir im Dorf ankamen. Niemand war auf der Straße. Nur ein Fenster war
erleuchtet, das des Bäckers, der schon für den nächsten Tag arbeitete. Wir klopften an
die Tür. „Kommen Sie herein“, sagte er, „draußen ist es kalt.“

Calava la notte quando arrivammo al paese. Non c'era nessuno per la strada. Solo una
finestra era illuminata, quella del fornaio, che lavorava già per il giorno dopo.

One, two, three. Un, deux, trois. Um, dois, três. Uno, dos, tres. Eins, zwei, drei. Uno,

And so they went home, and the lamp was put out, and that was the end of the day.
