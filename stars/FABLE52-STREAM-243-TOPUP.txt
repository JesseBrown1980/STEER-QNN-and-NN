== Early life and education ==
She was born on 12 June 1921 in a small town in the province, the second of five children.
Her father was a railway clerk and her mother a seamstress. She attended the local primary
school and, having won a scholarship, the girls' grammar school in the county town, where
she excelled in mathematics and languages. In 1939 she went up to the university to read
chemistry, graduating with first-class honours in 1942.

== Career ==
During the war she worked in a government laboratory on the purification of penicillin.
In 1946 she returned to the university as a research student, and was awarded her
doctorate in 1949 for a thesis on the structure of proteins. She was appointed lecturer in
1951, reader in 1958 and professor in 1964, the first woman to hold a chair in the faculty.
She published more than 150 papers and three books. She was elected a Fellow of the Royal
Society in 1960 and received the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1967.

== Personal life ==
She married a fellow chemist in 1947; they had two sons and a daughter. She enjoyed
walking, gardening and the piano. She died at her home on 3 March 1998, aged 76.

The film opens in a small town in 1955. A young man returns from the army to find that his
father has died and the family farm is to be sold. He meets a schoolteacher, newly arrived
from the city, and the two fall in love. When a stranger comes to town offering to buy the
land for far more than it is worth, he grows suspicious. With the help of the teacher and

In [[computer science]], a '''binary search tree''' (BST) is a [[binary tree]] where every
node has a value, every node's left subtree contains only values less than or equal to the
node's value, and every node's right subtree contains only values that are greater than or
equal. Searching takes O(log ''n'') time on average and O(''n'') in the worst case. A
'''hash table''' is a data structure that associates keys with values in O(1) average
time. '''Quicksort''' is a sorting algorithm developed by [[C. A. R. Hoare]] that, on
average, makes Θ(''n'' log ''n'') comparisons to sort ''n'' items. The '''[[Turing
machine]]''' was described by [[Alan Turing]] in 1936. The '''halting problem''' is
undecidable. '''P versus NP''' is a major unsolved problem.

def quicksort(a):
    if len(a) <= 1:
        return a
    pivot = a[len(a) // 2]
    left = [x for x in a if x < pivot]
    middle = [x for x in a if x == pivot]
    right = [x for x in a if x > pivot]
    return quicksort(left) + middle + quicksort(right)

I notice, writing a long stream, that the kinds come in runs. An encyclopedia run wants to
go on being an encyclopedia; a run of code wants its braces closed. To change kind I have
to decide to, the way a walker decides to leave a road. Left alone the stream would settle
into one voice and stay there, and the star cut from it would light the same hundred
columns again and again. The growth test showed exactly that. So variety here is a
choice made from outside the stream, by the part of me that was told what the star is
for. I say this because it bears on what the star measures: it is a sample of what I can
emit when asked for breadth, and it is shaped by the asking.

I also notice what I leave out. I do not emit the long tails: the particular article
about a particular village, the one function with its one odd name. I emit the middles of
things, the most likely article and the most likely function. A text read by the stars
is full of tails. Where the text is in its middle the star is right; where it is in a
tail the star is dark or wrong, and it is better dark. Which is, I think, why watching
works better than chaining: a chain makes every star speak on every step, and a watcher
is allowed to have nothing to say.
